Key Finding

The high correlations are among Dairy, Cereals, and Fruits & Veg:

  • Dairy–Cereals: 0.9420
  • Dairy–FruitVeg: 0.9171
  • Cereals–FruitVeg: 0.9575

This confirms the 0.92–0.96 range stated in our presentation. All three pairs rose together during 2019–2023, making it statistically difficult to isolate individual price effects for these groups.

Meat has lower correlations with other groups (0.35–0.51), which is why our Meat elasticity estimates are more reliable.

Method

Pearson correlation coefficients computed from the natural log of group prices (ln p₁, ln p₂, ln p₃, ln p₄) across all 27 province-year observations. Data source: Statistics Canada Table 18-10-0245-01, processed into annual group averages.

2. Cross-Price Elasticity η₁₄ = +1.22 Slide 36

This is our central finding: when Fruit & Vegetable prices rise by 10%, Meat demand increases by 12.2%. Below is the complete step-by-step calculation.

ηᵢⱼ = (γᵢⱼ − βᵢ · w̄ⱼ) / w̄ᵢ

Input Parameters

ParameterSymbolValueSource
F&V price coefficient in Meat equationγ₁₄0.322095OLS regression output (p < 0.01)
Expenditure coefficient, Meat equationβ₁0.009222OLS regression output
Mean Meat budget sharew̄₁0.2618Panel mean (27 observations)
Mean F&V budget sharew̄₄0.3214Panel mean (27 observations)

Step-by-Step Calculation

Step 1: β₁ × w̄₄ = 0.009222 × 0.3214 = 0.002964
Step 2: γ₁₄ − β₁·w̄₄ = 0.322095 − 0.002964 = 0.319131
Step 3: η₁₄ = 0.319131 / 0.2618 = 1.2189
η₁₄ = +1.22

(rounded to 2 decimal places)

Interpretation

When Fruit & Vegetable prices rise by 10%, Meat demand increases by 12.2%.

The positive sign (+) confirms these groups are substitutes, not complements. This is the strongest cross-category substitution effect in our 4×4 elasticity matrix, and directly answers our research question.
